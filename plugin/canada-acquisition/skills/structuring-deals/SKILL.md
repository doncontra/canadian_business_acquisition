# Structuring Deals

Produces an optimal deal structure for a Canadian acquisition — tailored to the seller's psychology, the deal economics, and available Canadian financing. Structure the stack in the right order: senior debt and seller paper fund the purchase, targeted founder programs can reduce the equity gap, and grants usually support post-close execution rather than purchase price. See [reference.md](reference.md) for structure templates, current financing sources, and Canadian legal considerations.

## Before you start: ask, then tailor

Good answers depend on who the buyer is and what the deal looks like. Don't research or draft until you have the facts below.

1. Use what the user has already said in this conversation, including answers given to another skill. Never ask for it again.
2. Ask for what's missing in one message: no more than 6 questions, numbered, most important first. Give example answers so each is quick to reply to. Use an ask-question tool if one is available.
3. Only ask what would change the answer. Skip anything with a sensible default.
4. If the user has already given everything, skip the questions and start.
5. If the user says to go ahead without answering, proceed and list your assumptions at the top of the output.
6. Start the output with a one-line recap of the facts you're working from, so the user can correct them.

Identity questions (age, self-identified groups) are optional. Say they are only used to find programs the buyer qualifies for.

Ask about:

- Purchase price, normalized EBITDA, and asset or share purchase
- Cash available, and lenders you've already talked to
- Whether the seller is open to a vendor take-back or an earnout
- Optional profile details for program eligibility (see sourcing-capital → Step 1)
- How much personal risk you'll take, such as a personal guarantee

## Workflow

- [ ] Confirm purchase price and normalized EBITDA (feeds debt service coverage)
- [ ] Assess seller motivation and preferred outcome (lump sum vs. payments?)
- [ ] Identify financing sources available (BDC, bank, seller, targeted founder program, equity partner)
- [ ] Design capital stack (layers from most to least senior)
- [ ] Calculate DSCR: Normalized EBITDA ÷ Annual Debt Service ≥ 1.25x
- [ ] Check whether the buyer profile unlocks targeted programs without weakening the core structure
- [ ] Illustrate seller's after-tax proceeds under different structures (deferred consideration benefit)
- [ ] Produce 2–3 structure options ranked by feasibility
- [ ] Identify legal and tax steps (share vs. asset, Section 167 election, Section 116 if non-resident)

## Canadian Capital Stack (build bottom to top)

```
┌─────────────────────────────────┐
│   Grants / Wage / Advisory      │  Indirect support, rarely purchase price
├─────────────────────────────────┤
│  Targeted founder programs      │  Companion capital, not core debt
├─────────────────────────────────┤
│     Seller VTB / Earnout        │  Least expensive, most flexible
├─────────────────────────────────┤
│     Buyer Equity (down payment) │
├─────────────────────────────────┤
│     BDC / Bank Acquisition Loan │  Government-backed or chartered bank
├─────────────────────────────────┤
│     Business Cash Flow (Annuity)│  Pay seller directly from EBITDA
└─────────────────────────────────┘
```

## Profile-Aware Rule

Use the buyer profile (sourcing-capital → "Step 1: Build the buyer profile") to test profile-based programs before finalizing the structure. Only include programs the buyer qualifies for, and never assume a profile the user has not stated:

- **Futurpreneur** (up to $75K, co-lent with BDC) if the buyer is 18–39 and needs companion capital plus a mentor
- **Black Entrepreneurship Loan Fund (FACE)** or bank Black entrepreneur loans if the buyer self-identifies as Black
- **AWE, WELF, Indigenous financial institutions, and Community Futures (rural)** where the profile fits. See sourcing-capital → reference.md → Program Matrix

Check each program's "Buying a business?" column before counting it toward the purchase price.

Do not let targeted programs distort the deal. If the core structure does not work with BDC, bank debt, seller paper, and DSCR, the deal is not fixed by a grant or founder program.

## Deferred Consideration (Key Canadian Advantage)

Canadian sellers pay capital gains tax **only as payments are received** — not all at once. A seller receiving payments over 10 years pays tax on each installment, dramatically increasing their after-tax proceeds. Always illustrate this vs. all-cash at close when pitching creative structures.

## DSCR Check

```
DSCR = Normalized EBITDA ÷ Annual Debt Service (principal + interest)
Minimum: 1.25x | Preferred: 1.5x+
```

## Output

Recommended capital stack with dollar amounts, DSCR analysis, seller after-tax comparison (lump sum vs. deferred), 2 alternative structures, legal/tax next steps, and a note identifying which supports are direct acquisition capital versus indirect post-close support.
