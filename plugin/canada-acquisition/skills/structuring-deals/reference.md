# Structuring Deals — Canadian Reference

## 2026 Structuring Update

- As of 28 May 2026, most Canadian deals should still be structured around **BDC or bank debt**, **seller VTB**, and **buyer equity**.
- Use **targeted founder programs** such as Futurpreneur, BELF, or AWE as companion capital, not as the primary purchase-price source.
- Use grants, wage subsidies, and regional support to strengthen the business around closing, not to underwrite goodwill.

## Core Structure Templates

### Structure A: BDC / Bank Deal

```
Purchase Price:     $2,000,000
BDC / Bank Loan:    $1,400,000 (70%)
Buyer Down Payment: $400,000 (20%)
Seller VTB Note:    $200,000 (10%) @ 6% over 5 years
Seller at Close:    $1,800,000
```

Timeline: 90–120 days. Good for strong businesses with assets or recurring revenue.

### Structure B: Seller-Finance Heavy (Low Cash In)

```
Purchase Price:     $1,500,000
Buyer Down:         $150,000 (10%)
Bank Loan:          $750,000 (50%)
Seller VTB:         $600,000 (40%) @ 6% over 5 years
Seller at Close:    $900,000
```

Good for motivated sellers. Seller defers tax, nets more overall.

### Structure C: Annuity Deal (Zero Down, No Bank)

```
Purchase Price:     $1,800,000
Seller at Close:    $0
Monthly Payments:   $15,000/month for 10 years = $1,800,000 total
Funded by:          Business cash flow (EBITDA)
```

Closes in days, not months. Best for retiring baby boomers needing monthly income.

### Structure D: Equity Partner + BDC

```
Purchase Price:     $3,000,000
BDC Loan:           $2,100,000 (70%)
Equity Partner:     $600,000 (20%) → receives 30% equity
Buyer Capital:      $300,000 (10%) → retains 70% equity
Seller at Close:    $3,000,000
```

### Structure E: Hybrid Creative

```
Purchase Price:     $2,500,000
BDC Loan:           $1,500,000 (60%)
Seller VTB:         $500,000 (20%) — starts year 2
Earnout:            $250,000 (10%) — Year 1 EBITDA target
Buyer Down:         $250,000 (10%)
Seller at Close:    $1,750,000
```

### Structure F: Profile-Aware Companion Capital

```
Purchase Price:     $1,800,000
BDC Loan:           $1,080,000 (60%)
Seller VTB:         $360,000 (20%)
Buyer Down:         $180,000 (10%)
BELF / targeted loan: $180,000 (10%)
Seller at Close:    $1,440,000
```

Useful when the buyer is Black and needs to close a modest equity gap without overusing expensive equity capital.

## Canadian Financing Sources

| Source                                            | Amount          | Key Feature                                                                        |
| ------------------------------------------------- | --------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| BDC (bdc.ca)                                      | $100K–$10M+     | Cash flow lending, no hard asset requirement, entrepreneur-friendly                |
| CSBFP                                             | Up to $1.15M    | Government-guaranteed; asset purchases only, never shares. Goodwill + working capital capped at $150K |
| Chartered banks (RBC, TD, Scotia, BMO, CIBC, ATB) | Varies          | 20–30% equity required, DSCR 1.25x+                                                |
| Black Entrepreneurship Loan Fund (BELF)           | Program-based   | Companion debt option for eligible Black entrepreneurs                             |
| Community Futures                                 | Region-specific | Smaller-market and rural lending plus advisory support                             |
| Seller VTB                                        | 10–40% of deal  | Deferred consideration = seller pays tax on receipt, not at once                   |
| Equity partner                                    | Negotiated      | Investor gets equity stake and preferred return                                    |

**CSBFP limitation**: Asset purchases of a going concern only; share purchases are not eligible. Goodwill, other intangibles, and working capital are capped at $150K combined, so on a goodwill-heavy deal CSBFP covers the equipment, leaseholds, and real estate while BDC or a bank carries the goodwill.

**Targeted program limitation**: Futurpreneur, BELF, AWE, and other profile-based programs can improve access to capital, but they do not replace lender underwriting, seller confidence, or DSCR.

## Earnout Best Practices (Canada)

- Keep metric simple: revenue or EBITDA only
- Cap period at 2–3 years
- Buyer must control the business during earnout period
- Define measurement methodology precisely in Purchase Agreement
- Canadian courts have upheld earnout provisions — document carefully

## Canadian Legal Structure Considerations

- **Share Sale**: Seller pays capital gains, eligible for LCGE (~$1.25M tax-free). Buyer inherits all historical liabilities.
- **Asset Sale**: Buyer gets clean start + asset step-up. Joint Section 167 election → no HST on going concern transfer.
- **Non-compete**: Enforceable if reasonable in scope and geography (2–5 years, same region).
- **Section 116 (Non-Resident)**: Buyer withholds 25% of price unless CRA clearance certificate in hand.
- **Quebec Employment Law**: Distinct provincial standards apply — review with a Quebec lawyer.

## Structure Selection Guide

| Situation                                                 | Likely Best Structure                                       |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Strong business, seller wants cash                        | BDC or bank-backed deal                                     |
| Seller wants tax efficiency and steady retirement income  | Seller-finance-heavy or annuity-style structure             |
| Buyer fits a targeted program and has a modest equity gap | Senior debt plus seller note plus companion founder capital |
| Buyer has limited cash but strong operator credibility    | Equity partner plus lender plus seller note                 |
| Earnings uncertain                                        | Lower fixed price plus earnout                              |
| Business not ready yet                                    | Consulting-for-equity or staged buy-in                      |

## What to Show the Seller

- Cash at close
- Total headline price
- Monthly or annual payment stream if deferred
- Approximate after-tax outcome under lump sum versus deferred consideration
- Why the structure makes the deal more likely to close

## Direct Versus Indirect Supports

- **Direct**: BDC, banks, seller VTB, CSBFP-backed asset loans, BELF, Community Futures loans
- **Indirect**: wage subsidies, training grants, digital adoption support, regional productivity funding, export support

Use indirect support to make the first 12 months safer, but do not present it as committed acquisition capital in the initial stack.
