# Financial Due Diligence — Canadian Reference

## Documents to Request

**Primary (must have before proceeding):**

- 3 years T2 Corporate Income Tax Returns (filed with CRA)
- 3 years financial statements (income statement, balance sheet, cash flow)
- Last 12 months of bank statements (all accounts)
- Accounts receivable aging report
- Accounts payable aging report
- Asset list (equipment, vehicles, inventory) with age and condition
- Lease agreements
- Top 10 customers by revenue (last 12 months)
- Employee list with compensation

**Secondary (request after primary review):**

- HST/GST filing history (confirms revenue — cross-check against reported revenue)
- WSIB (Ontario) or WCB (BC/AB) account status
- CRA correspondence or notices of assessment
- Shareholder agreements
- Any pending litigation

## Common Canadian Add-Backs

- Owner salary above fair market replacement (e.g., owner pays self $300K but a GM costs $120K → add back $180K)
- Family members on payroll not providing real value
- Personal vehicle, gas, insurance through business
- Personal travel and meals
- Non-arm's-length rent (owner charges business rent from related entity)
- One-time legal/accounting fees
- CRA-adjusted expenses from prior audits

## Revenue Quality Tiers

| Type                                   | Quality   | Notes                          |
| -------------------------------------- | --------- | ------------------------------ |
| Multi-year contracted recurring        | Excellent | Highest valuation multiple     |
| Annual recurring (auto-renewals)       | Very Good | Watch churn rate               |
| Repeat transactional (loyal customers) | Good      | Verify repeat rate             |
| Project-based (one-time)               | Moderate  | Pipeline risk if work dries up |
| Single customer >25% revenue           | Risk      | Apply concentration discount   |

## Canadian Tax Considerations

- **Share Sale vs. Asset Sale**: Sellers prefer share sales (Lifetime Capital Gains Exemption up to ~$1.25M tax-free for QSBC shares). Buyers prefer asset sales (step up asset values, no inherited liabilities). This is a key negotiation point.
- **HST on Asset Sales**: Jointly elect under Section 167 of the Excise Tax Act to treat as going concern — no HST on qualifying asset transfers.
- **Section 116 (Non-Resident Seller)**: If seller is a non-resident of Canada, buyer must withhold 25% of purchase price and remit to CRA unless a clearance certificate is obtained first.
- **CRA Clearance Certificate**: Request early — can take 4–6 weeks. Required to confirm no outstanding tax obligations before close.

## Deal Breaker Financials

- Revenue/EBITDA cannot be confirmed against T2 returns or bank statements
- Unexplained large cash withdrawals
- AR aged >90 days with no explanation
- CRA liens or unpaid payroll/HST remittances
- True normalized EBITDA materially lower than seller represented (>15% variance)

## Validation Tests

- Compare reported revenue to bank deposits and HST/GST filings
- Compare payroll expense to employee list and remittance records
- Check whether gross margins are stable or drifting for a real reason
- Confirm add-backs are documented and truly non-recurring
- Separate maintenance capex from growth capex before sizing debt capacity

## Standard Due Diligence Output

1. Normalized EBITDA bridge
2. Revenue quality rating
3. Working capital snapshot
4. Free cash flow estimate
5. Top financial risks
6. Recommended valuation adjustment, if any
