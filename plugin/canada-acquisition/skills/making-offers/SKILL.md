# Making Offers

Produces a complete LOI draft and verbal presentation script for a Canadian acquisition offer. See [reference.md](reference.md) for the LOI template, Canadian-specific clauses, and negotiation strategy.

## Before you start: ask, then tailor

Good answers depend on who the buyer is and what the deal looks like. Don't research or draft until you have the facts below.

1. Use what the user has already said in this conversation, including answers given to another skill. Never ask for it again.
2. Ask for what's missing in one message: no more than 6 questions, numbered, most important first. Give example answers so each is quick to reply to. Use an ask-question tool if one is available.
3. Only ask what would change the answer. Skip anything with a sensible default.
4. If the user has already given everything, skip the questions and start.
5. If the user says to go ahead without answering, proceed and list your assumptions at the top of the output.
6. Start the output with a one-line recap of the facts you're working from, so the user can correct them.

Ask about:

- Normalized EBITDA, your valuation range, and the proposed structure
- What the seller has said about price, terms, and timing
- Financing plan and cash available
- Asset or share purchase, and province
- First offer or a counter-offer

## Workflow

- [ ] Confirm: normalized EBITDA, valuation range, proposed deal structure, seller motivation level
- [ ] Set offer price (floor, target, or ceiling based on motivation)
- [ ] Design payment terms (cash at close + VTB + earnout as needed)
- [ ] Select share sale vs. asset sale preference
- [ ] Set exclusivity period (45–60 days standard in Canada)
- [ ] Define conditions (due diligence, financing, lease assignment, CRA clearance)
- [ ] Set transition period terms (60–90 days typical, paid)
- [ ] Draft non-compete terms (2–5 years, same geographic area)
- [ ] Draft cover note / verbal script tailored to seller archetype

## Offer Pricing by Motivation

| Seller Motivation | Opening Price Strategy |
|---|---|
| Very high (score 40+) | At or below floor — room to negotiate up |
| Medium (25–39) | At target — strong terms offset lower price |
| Low (below 25) | At or near ceiling — needs to be compelling |

**Price is only one variable.** A lower price with a share sale structure that maximizes the seller's LCGE exemption often nets the seller more than a higher all-cash asset sale.

## Key Canadian clause to include

Always reference that closing is conditional on no outstanding CRA liens or assessments — or resolution of same. And for asset sales, reference the joint Section 167 election (no HST on going concern transfer).

## Output

Offer summary, pricing rationale, structure rationale, completed LOI draft, negotiation guidance (what to hold firm / what to concede), verbal cover script.
