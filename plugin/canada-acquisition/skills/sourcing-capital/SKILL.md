# Sourcing Capital

Matches a specific Canadian acquisition to the right financing sources for this buyer, and prepares the user to approach lenders. Keep the hierarchy clear: debt finances the purchase, seller paper reduces cash down, and grants usually support diligence, hiring, training, productivity, or post-close growth rather than the acquisition price itself. See [reference.md](reference.md) for the program matrix, lender details, borrower package checklist, and DSCR calculation.

## Before you start: ask, then tailor

Good answers depend on who the buyer is and what the deal looks like. Don't research or draft until you have the facts below.

1. Use what the user has already said in this conversation, including answers given to another skill. Never ask for it again.
2. Ask for what's missing in one message: no more than 6 questions, numbered, most important first. Give example answers so each is quick to reply to. Use an ask-question tool if one is available.
3. Only ask what would change the answer. Skip anything with a sensible default.
4. If the user has already given everything, skip the questions and start.
5. If the user says to go ahead without answering, proceed and list your assumptions at the top of the output.
6. Start the output with a one-line recap of the facts you're working from, so the user can correct them.

Identity questions (age, self-identified groups) are optional. Say they are only used to find programs the buyer qualifies for.

Ask about: the buyer-profile fields in Step 1 below.

## Step 1: Build the buyer profile

Program eligibility depends on who the buyer is, not only on the deal. Before recommending programs, collect the profile below. Skip anything the user has already said. Ask the rest in one short message, and tell the user every identity question is optional and only used to find programs they qualify for.

| Field | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Province, and city or rural town where the business is | Provincial programs, ATB, credit unions, Community Futures (rural only) |
| Age | Futurpreneur (18–39) |
| Citizenship: citizen, permanent resident, or other | Most programs require citizen or PR |
| Self-identified groups, optional: woman, Black, Indigenous (First Nations, Métis, Inuit), newcomer, person with a disability, 2SLGBTQI+, veteran | Targeted loan funds and bank programs |
| Cash available for down payment, and other assets for collateral | Equity requirement, AWE and Community Futures collateral rules |
| Credit score range | Bank appetite |
| Industry experience and management experience | Lender comfort on a first acquisition |
| First acquisition or repeat buyer | BDC vs. bank path |
| Deal: price, SDE or EBITDA, asset or share purchase, equipment and real estate included | CSBFP fit, DSCR, collateral |

Then filter reference.md → Program Matrix by the profile. Only show programs the buyer is eligible for, plus the general programs that fit everyone. Never assume a profile the user has not stated.

## Step 2: Workflow

- [ ] Confirm deal size, structure (asset or share), and normalized EBITDA
- [ ] Calculate DSCR (EBITDA ÷ annual debt service). Must be ≥ 1.25x for any Canadian lender
- [ ] Assess BDC fit (cash flow lending, finances goodwill and share purchases)
- [ ] Assess CSBFP fit: asset purchase of a going concern only, never shares. Term loan up to $1M; of the $500K for equipment and leaseholds, at most $150K can cover goodwill, other intangibles, and working capital
- [ ] Screen the targeted programs the profile qualifies for, and note whether each one allows buying an existing business
- [ ] Separate direct acquisition financing from indirect support so the user does not rely on grants for purchase price
- [ ] Identify the top 2–3 banks or credit unions for this deal type and province
- [ ] Check Community Futures (rural) and regional agencies for post-close capex, hiring, or smaller loans
- [ ] Quantify seller VTB opportunity. Pitch the deferred tax benefit to the seller
- [ ] Identify if an equity partner is needed for the down payment
- [ ] Build lender package checklist (borrower + business + deal documents)

## DSCR Formula

```
DSCR = Normalized EBITDA ÷ Annual Debt Service
Minimum: 1.25x | Target: 1.5x+
```

Example: $500K EBITDA ÷ $280K annual loan payments = **1.79x DSCR** ✅

## Program Tiers

| Tier   | What belongs here | Use in acquisition planning |
| ------ | ----------------- | --------------------------- |
| Tier 1 | BDC acquisition lending, bank and credit union term loans, CSBFP (asset deals), seller VTB | Primary sources for purchase price and closing funds |
| Tier 2 | Profile-based loan funds: Futurpreneur, FACE/BELF, bank Black entrepreneur loans, AWE, WELF, Indigenous financial institutions, Community Futures | Small companion capital to close an equity gap, plus mentorship. Usually $25K–$250K, so never the main lender on a $500K+ deal |
| Tier 3 | Regional development agencies, wage subsidies, training or productivity grants | After close or alongside closing, for equipment, hiring, digital projects, or expansion |

## Rules for targeted programs

- Many identity-based loans are written for starting or growing a business. Check reference.md → "Buying a business?" before counting one toward the purchase price. If it's unclear, tell the user to ask the lender directly.
- They improve a stack only if the deal already qualifies on fundamentals. Lead with DSCR and the business, not the program.
- Program terms change often. Cite reference.md's "last checked" date and tell the user to confirm with the lender before relying on a number.

## BDC vs. Bank Quick Guide

|                     | BDC                 | Chartered Bank      |
| ------------------- | ------------------- | ------------------- |
| Equity required     | 10–20% (flexible)   | 20–30% (firm)       |
| Cash flow lending   | Yes, primary basis  | Prefers hard assets |
| First-time acquirer | Friendly            | More skeptical      |
| Timeline            | 45–90 days          | 60–120 days         |
| Rate                | Prime + 1.5–3.5%    | Prime + 1–3%        |

**Start with BDC** for most Canadian acquisitions, especially service businesses without heavy assets.

## Output

- Buyer profile used (and which fields were not provided)
- Programs the buyer qualifies for, split into direct acquisition capital and indirect support, each with amount, whether it can fund a purchase, and a link
- Programs checked and ruled out, with the reason
- Recommended capital stack, DSCR analysis, and seller VTB opportunity
- Bank or credit union shortlist with rationale
- Lender package checklist, outreach order, and estimated timeline
