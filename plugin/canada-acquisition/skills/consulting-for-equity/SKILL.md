# Consulting for Equity

Helps the buyer enter a Canadian business through value creation first and ownership second. See [reference.md](reference.md) for structure examples, milestone ideas, seller framing, and legal cautions.

## Before you start: ask, then tailor

Good answers depend on who the buyer is and what the deal looks like. Don't research or draft until you have the facts below.

1. Use what the user has already said in this conversation, including answers given to another skill. Never ask for it again.
2. Ask for what's missing in one message: no more than 6 questions, numbered, most important first. Give example answers so each is quick to reply to. Use an ask-question tool if one is available.
3. Only ask what would change the answer. Skip anything with a sensible default.
4. If the user has already given everything, skip the questions and start.
5. If the user says to go ahead without answering, proceed and list your assumptions at the top of the output.
6. Start the output with a one-line recap of the facts you're working from, so the user can correct them.

Ask about:

- The business, and why it isn't ready to sell today
- The owner's goals, age, and timeline
- Your skills that would move the business forward
- Cash available, and whether you need income while you earn in
- Hours per week you can commit

## When to use this

- The business is attractive but not yet financeable
- The seller wants help before exiting
- The buyer has skill but limited down payment capital
- The company has fixable transfer-of-value issues
- The buyer wants a path to ownership without buying 100% on day one

## Workflow

- [ ] Diagnose why the business is not sale-ready
- [ ] Identify the 3-5 highest-value improvements the buyer can lead
- [ ] Choose structure: advisory equity, staged buy-in, management buyout, or option to acquire later
- [ ] Define milestones tied to equity vesting or purchase rights
- [ ] Set governance: decision rights, reporting, bank authority, compensation
- [ ] Protect downside with written agreements, exit rights, and trigger events
- [ ] Map the path from sweat equity to full acquisition or sale

## Common structures

**Advisory equity**: buyer receives minority equity for a defined growth or operational mandate.

**Buy-in management buyout**: buyer joins operations, improves the business, and earns the right to buy the seller out later.

**Option structure**: buyer gets an option to acquire additional shares after hitting agreed milestones.

**Hybrid cash plus sweat**: small cash component plus operational value creation and seller carry.

## Canada-specific issues

- Confirm whether the business is a share sale candidate or should remain an asset deal later
- Clarify GST/HST, payroll, and CRA compliance before the buyer takes operational responsibility
- Use a Canadian M&A lawyer to document vesting, defaults, deadlock rules, and buy-sell rights
- Be careful where provincial licensing or regulated professional status is required to control operations

## Output

Recommend the best consulting-for-equity structure, milestone plan, equity split logic, key legal protections, seller talking points, and the path to a later buyout.
