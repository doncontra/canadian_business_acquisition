# Analyzing Seller Psychology

Classifies sellers by archetype, scores their motivation, and produces a tailored approach and deal structure recommendation. See [reference.md](reference.md) for Canadian-specific archetypes, scoring matrix, and discovery questions.

## Before you start: ask, then tailor

Good answers depend on who the buyer is and what the deal looks like. Don't research or draft until you have the facts below.

1. Use what the user has already said in this conversation, including answers given to another skill. Never ask for it again.
2. Ask for what's missing in one message: no more than 6 questions, numbered, most important first. Give example answers so each is quick to reply to. Use an ask-question tool if one is available.
3. Only ask what would change the answer. Skip anything with a sensible default.
4. If the user has already given everything, skip the questions and start.
5. If the user says to go ahead without answering, proceed and list your assumptions at the top of the output.
6. Start the output with a one-line recap of the facts you're working from, so the user can correct them.

Ask about:

- What you know about the owner: age, health, family, years owned
- What they've said about why they're selling and their timeline
- On-market through a broker or off-market
- How many conversations you've had, and how they went
- What you need: how to approach them, which structure they'll accept, or how to build rapport

## Workflow

- [ ] Identify seller archetype (retiring boomer, burnt out, health-motivated, partnership dispute, etc.)
- [ ] Score motivation across 6 dimensions (1–10 each, max 60)
- [ ] Assess deal risk: who else is in the decision? Any blockers?
- [ ] Recommend approach: tone, talking points, what to emphasize
- [ ] Recommend deal structure that fits their psychology
- [ ] Flag any red flags (unrealistic price, multiple failed deals, evasive on financials)

## Core principle

Find **distressed sellers of good businesses** — not distressed businesses. Personal circumstances create the motivation, not business problems.

**High motivation (score 40+)**: Move fast, creative structure possible, below-market multiple achievable.
**Medium (25–39)**: Build relationship, create urgency around deferred tax benefit.
**Low (below 25)**: Stay in touch, don't prioritize.

## Key Canadian insight

Canadian sellers respond strongly to the **deferred consideration tax advantage** — they only pay capital gains tax as they receive payments (not all at once). Frame seller financing as a financial benefit to them, not a concession from you.

## Output

Given seller info, produce: archetype, motivation score, deal risk, recommended approach + talking points, suggested deal structure.
