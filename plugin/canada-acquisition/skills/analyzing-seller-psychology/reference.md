# Analyzing Seller Psychology — Canadian Reference

## Canadian Seller Archetypes

| Archetype                  | Signals                                              | Your Approach                                                              |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Retiring Baby Boomer**   | Age 58–75, most net worth in business, low RRSP      | Be the safe pair of hands. Show commitment to staff and legacy.            |
| **Burnt Out Owner**        | 10–20 years in, tired, COVID was the final straw     | Acknowledge what they've been through. Sell them their freedom.            |
| **Health-Motivated**       | Illness or health event, high urgency                | Move quickly, be empathetic, negotiate structure not price.                |
| **Partnership Dispute**    | Co-founders in conflict, wants a clean break         | Position as the clean exit. Verify who has authority to sell.              |
| **Bored / Ready for Next** | Business doing fine, owner lost passion              | Be a peer. Ask about their next venture. Make the sale easy.               |
| **No Succession Plan**     | No family/employee to take over, worried about staff | Emphasize your commitment to retaining the team and honoring their legacy. |

## Motivation Scoring Matrix

Score each dimension 1–10:

| Dimension           | Low (1–3)                | Medium (4–6)    | High (7–10)                |
| ------------------- | ------------------------ | --------------- | -------------------------- |
| Urgency             | No timeline              | Vague "someday" | Specific event/date        |
| Financial need      | Wealthy, doesn't need it | Some need       | Retirement depends on it   |
| Emotional readiness | Very attached            | Mixed           | Ready to move on           |
| Business health     | Declining                | Stable          | Growing                    |
| Successor           | Has one                  | Unclear         | No plan at all             |
| External pressure   | None                     | Some            | Health / divorce / partner |

**Total: 40–60 = high motivation | 25–39 = medium | <25 = low**

## Discovery Questions (First Seller Call)

1. "How long have you been thinking about this transition?"
2. "What does your ideal timeline look like?"
3. "What's most important to you — price, speed, or what happens to your team?"
4. "Have you spoken to your accountant or lawyer about this yet?"
5. "What would you do with your time after the sale?"
6. "Is anyone else involved in this decision?" (spouse, partner, family)
7. "Have you had any previous conversations with buyers or brokers?"

## Canadian-Specific Seller Considerations

- **Lifetime Capital Gains Exemption (LCGE)**: Up to ~$1.25M tax-free on qualifying small business shares (QSBC). Sellers are very motivated by share-sale structures that preserve this.
- **Deferred consideration**: Sellers pay capital gains tax only as payments are received — not all at once. A major incentive for seller financing. Frame it as: "You could net significantly more after tax by taking structured payments."
- **Quebec sellers**: More relationship-driven culture; direct deal pitches less effective; French language matters.
- **Rural sellers**: Deep community ties; care deeply about local legacy and staff.
- **Privacy**: Canadian sellers are often very private about selling — lead with discretion.

## Red Flags

- Seller is not the actual decision-maker
- Unrealistic price, won't discuss it
- Evasive about financials
- Wants all cash at close, zero flexibility
- Has had 3+ failed deals with previous buyers
- Emotionally unable to separate from the business

## Talking Point Priorities By Archetype

- Retiring boomer: legacy, staff continuity, reliable payments, tax efficiency
- Burnt out owner: speed, relief, reduced stress, practical certainty
- Health-motivated: empathy, simplicity, fast close path
- Partnership dispute: clean separation, clarity, low drama
- Bored owner: flexibility, next chapter, efficient transaction

## What To Listen For

- Timeline signals: retirement date, health event, partner tension, family pressure
- Identity signals: whether they see themselves as the business
- Money signals: whether price, speed, or certainty matters most
