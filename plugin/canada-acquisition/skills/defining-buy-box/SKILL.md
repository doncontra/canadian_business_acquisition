# Defining Buy Box

Produces a completed buy box for a Canadian acquisition buyer. See [reference.md](reference.md) for industry sweet spots, red flags, and a fillable template.

## Before you start: ask, then tailor

Good answers depend on who the buyer is and what the deal looks like. Don't research or draft until you have the facts below.

1. Use what the user has already said in this conversation, including answers given to another skill. Never ask for it again.
2. Ask for what's missing in one message: no more than 6 questions, numbered, most important first. Give example answers so each is quick to reply to. Use an ask-question tool if one is available.
3. Only ask what would change the answer. Skip anything with a sensible default.
4. If the user has already given everything, skip the questions and start.
5. If the user says to go ahead without answering, proceed and list your assumptions at the top of the output.
6. Start the output with a one-line recap of the facts you're working from, so the user can correct them.

Ask about:

- What's your professional background, and where do you have experience or a network?
- Where in Canada are you located or willing to operate?
- Owner-operator or owner-investor?
- How much cash can you put in, or do you need creative or low-money-down structures?
- One business or building a portfolio?
- Anything off-limits, such as licensed trades, franchises, or retail?

## Workflow

- [ ] Determine buyer type: owner-operator vs. owner-investor
- [ ] Identify industries where buyer has skills or network (stay in lane)
- [ ] Set geography: province, city, or remote-friendly
- [ ] Define deal size: revenue range, EBITDA range, purchase price range
- [ ] Determine financing approach: BDC, bank, seller finance, creative
- [ ] Identify if a partner is needed (2/3 of deals in acquisition communities are partner deals)
- [ ] Output completed buy box definition

## Key principles

**Stay in your lane.** Buyers who leverage existing industry knowledge, networks, and credibility close faster and negotiate better.

**Owner-operator** = buy near where you live, run it yourself. Good for first deal.
**Owner-investor** = hire a GM/operator, buy anywhere in Canada, scale to multiple businesses.

**Partner deals** are common and smart — combine operator skills with capital partner.

## Output

Completed Canadian buy box, recommended industries, acquisition style, financing posture, and the next sourcing step.
