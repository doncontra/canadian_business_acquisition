# Building Deal Teams — Canadian Reference

## Role Profiles

### Canadian M&A Lawyer

**What to look for:** Small-to-mid market M&A experience (not general corporate), familiar with province-specific corporate law (OBCA, BCBCA, ABCA), experience with BDC lending requirements, responsive.
**Where to find:** Law Society referral (by province), Lexpert (lexpert.ca), Chambers Canada rankings, referrals from your BDC advisor or CPA.

### CPA / Chartered Professional Accountant

**What to look for:** CPA designation (not just bookkeeper), QSBC / LCGE planning experience, share vs. asset sale tax analysis, post-acquisition accounting setup.
**Strong Canadian firms:** MNP, BDO, Grant Thornton, KPMG (for larger deals). All have dedicated deal advisory practices.
**Where to find:** CPA Canada directory (cpacanada.ca).

### BDC Advisor

BDC has business centres in every province. Book a meeting directly: bdc.ca/en/financing
Introduce yourself as an acquisition entrepreneur, not a startup founder. Bring your buy box and a target deal if you have one.

### Commercial Banker

Build a relationship before you have a deal in hand. Use this pitch:

> "I'm an acquisition entrepreneur. I'm pre-qualified and looking to buy a profitable [industry] business in [province]. I'm looking for a bank that wants to work with me on multiple deals over time."
> ATB Financial (Alberta only) and Meridian (Ontario) are most flexible for first-time acquirers.

### Operations Partner / GM

**Internal (best option):** Promote the existing #2 in the business — they know the business, customers trust them.
**External:** LinkedIn search "[industry] General Manager [province]", executive recruiting firms (Gallagher, Lee Hecht Harrison Canada), industry associations.
**Compensation:** Base salary + 5–15% equity vesting over 3–4 years, or base + EBITDA performance bonus.

### Equity / Capital Partner

**Where to find:**

- NACO (National Angel Capital Organization) — naco.ca
- Anges Québec (Quebec angel investors)
- LinkedIn: search "business acquisition investor Canada"
- Real estate investor communities (often want to diversify into businesses)
- Your professional network (accountants and lawyers often know capital-ready investors)

**Terms framework:**

- Investor contributes 100% of down payment
- Receives 20–40% equity + preferred return (8–10%/year on capital until repaid)
- Buyer operates, retains 60–80% equity
- Major decisions (exit, large capex, new debt) require both to agree

## Partner Deal Structure Template

```
PARTNER DEAL FRAMEWORK

Partner A (Operator):
  Contribution: Sourcing, due diligence, operations post-close
  Equity: [X]%
  Salary: $[X]/year post-close
  Role: CEO / General Manager

Partner B (Capital Partner):
  Contribution: $[X] down payment
  Equity: [X]%
  Return: [X]% preferred return until capital returned, then pro-rata distributions
  Role: Board observer / passive investor

Decision Making:
  Day-to-day ops: Partner A decides alone
  Expenditures >$[X]: Both partners agree
  Sale of business: Both partners agree
  New debt >$[X]: Both partners agree
```

## Team Build Sequence

1. Define buy box and target deal size
2. Build lender relationship before LOI if possible
3. Shortlist lawyer and CPA before serious diligence begins
4. Identify operator or partner gap before making offers on bigger deals
5. Add insurance, HR, or integration specialists only when the deal demands it

## Questions to Ask Any Advisor

- How many Canadian acquisition deals like this have you handled?
- Which provinces and industries are you strongest in?
- What usually kills deals at your stage of the process?
- What is your fee model and timeline responsiveness?
