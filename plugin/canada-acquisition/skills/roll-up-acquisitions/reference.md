# Roll-Up Acquisitions — Canadian Reference

## Platform Company Criteria

- EBITDA large enough to support reporting and leadership overhead
- Strong books and lender-ready financials
- Clear management depth or promotable second-in-command
- Sector with fragmentation and repeatable acquisition targets
- Province and regulatory profile suitable for expansion

## Bolt-On Filters

- Same customer type or adjacent service line
- Geography that adds route density or local coverage
- Easy back-office integration
- Owner willing to stay briefly for transition
- Purchase multiple below the platform's future exit multiple

## Typical Synergy Buckets

- Shared bookkeeping and admin
- Centralized marketing and lead handling
- Better purchasing power
- Shared technicians, vehicles, or route density
- Cross-sell into existing customer base
- Unified pricing and quoting discipline

## Suggested Sequence

1. Buy the platform
2. Stabilize reporting and integration cadence
3. Add easiest adjacent bolt-ons first
4. Add strategically valuable but harder targets later
5. Package the larger platform for PE or strategic exit

## Multiple Arbitrage Logic

- Buy small private businesses at 2.5x to 4.0x EBITDA
- Improve systems and scale the combined entity
- Exit the larger platform at a higher multiple if leadership, reporting, and transferability improve

## Integration Dashboard

Track weekly for each acquisition:

- Revenue retention
- Employee retention
- Customer churn
- Gross margin
- Synergy capture
- Integration tasks completed

## Strong Canadian Roll-Up Verticals

- Trades and home services
- Clinics and healthcare services
- Accounting, insurance, payroll, and compliance services
- Route-based B2B services
- Niche industrial and maintenance services

## Canadian Friction Points

- Province-by-province labour differences
- Licensing and permit transfer issues
- Quebec language and legal differences
- Integration strain if systems differ too much
- Underestimating leadership bandwidth after close
