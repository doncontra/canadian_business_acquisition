# Roll-Up Acquisitions

Builds a Canada-specific plan for consolidating multiple smaller companies into one more valuable platform. See [reference.md](reference.md) for platform criteria, bolt-on filters, synergy buckets, and integration guidance.

## Before you start: ask, then tailor

Good answers depend on who the buyer is and what the deal looks like. Don't research or draft until you have the facts below.

1. Use what the user has already said in this conversation, including answers given to another skill. Never ask for it again.
2. Ask for what's missing in one message: no more than 6 questions, numbered, most important first. Give example answers so each is quick to reply to. Use an ask-question tool if one is available.
3. Only ask what would change the answer. Skip anything with a sensible default.
4. If the user has already given everything, skip the questions and start.
5. If the user says to go ahead without answering, proceed and list your assumptions at the top of the output.
6. Start the output with a one-line recap of the facts you're working from, so the user can correct them.

Ask about:

- Sector and province
- Whether you already own a platform business
- Capital and partners available
- How many acquisitions over what timeline
- Hold long term, or build to sell

## Workflow

- [ ] Define the platform thesis: sector, geography, customer type, and why fragmentation creates opportunity
- [ ] Choose platform company criteria: leadership, systems, margins, financing readiness
- [ ] Define bolt-on criteria: size, location, customer overlap, labour fit, cross-sell potential
- [ ] Identify synergy buckets: back office, purchasing, sales, scheduling, branding, management
- [ ] Sequence deals: platform first, then easiest bolt-ons, then harder strategic add-ons
- [ ] Model multiple arbitrage: buy at small-business multiples, sell larger platform at higher multiple
- [ ] Create integration playbook and 100-day priorities after each acquisition
- [ ] Outline likely exit buyers: private equity, regional strategic, national consolidator

## Best-fit Canadian sectors

- HVAC, plumbing, electrical, roofing, restoration, and other home services
- Clinics, pharmacies, diagnostics, and non-physician healthcare services
- Accounting, insurance, wealth, and selected professional services
- B2B agencies, niche industrial services, and route-based service businesses

## Key principles

**Platform before sprawl**: do not buy random businesses. Start with one company strong enough to anchor reporting, integration, and leadership.

**Synergy must be specific**: shared admin, stronger pricing, cross-sell, route density, or procurement leverage.

**Exit is part of entry**: know who would buy the scaled platform before starting the roll-up.

## Canada-specific considerations

- Provincial expansion creates tax, labour, and licensing complexity; integration plans must reflect province-by-province rules
- Roll-ups often need a mix of senior debt, seller paper, and equity partners once they move beyond first-deal bank financing
- In regulated sectors, verify whether licences and key-person requirements survive post-close consolidation

## Output

Provide roll-up thesis, ideal platform profile, bolt-on scorecard, synergy map, acquisition sequence, financing view, and target exit path.
