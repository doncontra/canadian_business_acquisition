# Post-Acquisition Planning

Produces a 90-day action plan, value creation roadmap, and Canadian tax optimization checklist for a newly acquired business. See [reference.md](reference.md) for the weekly reporting dashboard, employee retention tactics, and exit planning framework.

## Before you start: ask, then tailor

Good answers depend on who the buyer is and what the deal looks like. Don't research or draft until you have the facts below.

1. Use what the user has already said in this conversation, including answers given to another skill. Never ask for it again.
2. Ask for what's missing in one message: no more than 6 questions, numbered, most important first. Give example answers so each is quick to reply to. Use an ask-question tool if one is available.
3. Only ask what would change the answer. Skip anything with a sensible default.
4. If the user has already given everything, skip the questions and start.
5. If the user says to go ahead without answering, proceed and list your assumptions at the top of the output.
6. Start the output with a one-line recap of the facts you're working from, so the user can correct them.

Ask about:

- Industry, closing date, and number of employees
- Key people the business depends on
- Your role day to day, and how long the seller will stay on
- Your biggest worry for the first 90 days
- Growth goals and how long you plan to own it

## Workflow

- [ ] **Week 1–2 (Stabilize)**: Announce to staff, call top customers and suppliers, review cash position, get digital access
- [ ] **Week 3–4 (Assess)**: Review financials, map processes, identify top employees and top customers, assess pipeline
- [ ] **Month 2–3 (Optimize)**: Implement reporting, review pricing, identify quick wins, begin process documentation
- [ ] Set up weekly KPI dashboard from day 1
- [ ] Engage CPA to optimize corporate structure, salary/dividend split, RRSP strategy
- [ ] Identify top 3 value creation levers specific to this business
- [ ] Draft 3–5 year exit strategy (private equity sale or roll-up)

## First rule: do no harm

Do not make major changes in the first 30 days. Employees fear uncertainty. Customers fear disruption. Earn trust before making moves.

## Highest-impact value creation levers

1. **Pricing** — Most Canadian SMEs are underpriced. 5–10% price increase often increases EBITDA 20–30%.
2. **Recurring revenue** — Convert transactional customers to contracts/subscriptions. Dramatically increases exit multiple.
3. **Owner-independence** — Develop your #2 into a true GM. Single biggest driver of multiple expansion.
4. **Digital presence** — Baby boomer-owned businesses typically have weak Google presence; easy wins.
5. **Roll-up** — Buy 1–2 competitors. Combined EBITDA attracts PE at 5x–8x vs. 3x you paid.

## Canadian tax optimization (engage CPA immediately)

- Corporate structure: confirm CCPC / QSBC status for future LCGE eligibility
- Salary vs. dividend split: optimize personal/corporate combined tax rate
- RRSP contributions: use business income to maximize
- Holding company: consider holdco above opco for asset protection and deal flexibility

## Output

90-day week-by-week action plan, employee risk assessment, top 3 value creation opportunities, CRA/tax checklist, KPI dashboard setup, exit strategy outline.
