# Canada LOI Review

Review a draft LOI or offer summary for a Canadian business acquisition.

## Before you start: ask, then tailor

Good answers depend on who the buyer is and what the deal looks like. Don't research or draft until you have the facts below.

1. Use what the user has already said in this conversation, including answers given to another skill. Never ask for it again.
2. Ask for what's missing in one message: no more than 6 questions, numbered, most important first. Give example answers so each is quick to reply to. Use an ask-question tool if one is available.
3. Only ask what would change the answer. Skip anything with a sensible default.
4. If the user has already given everything, skip the questions and start.
5. If the user says to go ahead without answering, proceed and list your assumptions at the top of the output.
6. Start the output with a one-line recap of the facts you're working from, so the user can correct them.

This workflow loads several skills. Ask one combined set of questions for the whole workflow; the loaded skills should not ask again.

Ask about:

- The draft LOI text or a summary of the terms (paste)
- Who drafted it, and whether you're sending it or received it
- What the seller has already agreed to verbally
- Your financing plan, and whether a lender has seen the terms
- Province, and asset or share purchase
- Your biggest concerns about the current draft

## Load these skills first

- `making-offers`
- `structuring-deals`
- `analyzing-seller-psychology`
- `closing-deals`

## Workflow

1. Ask for the draft LOI text or a summary of the proposed terms.
2. Review the economics: price, structure, seller paper, exclusivity, and transition terms.
3. Check whether the LOI matches seller psychology and realistic financing.
4. Identify missing Canadian protections such as CRA, Section 167, Section 116, working capital, lease, and non-compete issues.
5. Flag negotiation weaknesses or terms likely to create trouble later in diligence or closing.
6. Recommend revisions before sending or signing.

## Output

Provide:

- LOI strengths
- missing or weak clauses
- structure concerns
- financing concerns
- negotiation comments
- revised-term recommendations
