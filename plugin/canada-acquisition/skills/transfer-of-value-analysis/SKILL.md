# Transfer of Value Analysis

Assesses whether a Canadian business is worth more than its balance sheet by measuring how transferable its cash flow is to a new owner. See [reference.md](reference.md) for pillar scoring, threshold bands, and typical Canadian transfer risks.

## Before you start: ask, then tailor

Good answers depend on who the buyer is and what the deal looks like. Don't research or draft until you have the facts below.

1. Use what the user has already said in this conversation, including answers given to another skill. Never ask for it again.
2. Ask for what's missing in one message: no more than 6 questions, numbered, most important first. Give example answers so each is quick to reply to. Use an ask-question tool if one is available.
3. Only ask what would change the answer. Skip anything with a sensible default.
4. If the user has already given everything, skip the questions and start.
5. If the user says to go ahead without answering, proceed and list your assumptions at the top of the output.
6. Start the output with a one-line recap of the facts you're working from, so the user can correct them.

Ask about:

- Customers: top customer's share of revenue, and whether there are contracts
- Staff: key employees, tenure, and who holds the relationships
- The owner's role day to day
- Industry, and how you plan to run it

## Workflow

- [ ] Score customer value drivers: concentration, recurring revenue, contract length, switching costs
- [ ] Score employee value drivers: tenure, management depth, technical capability, key-person risk
- [ ] Score structural value drivers: SOPs, systems, KPIs, owner dependence, reporting quality
- [ ] Score social value drivers: reputation, reviews, referral strength, culture, brand position
- [ ] Calculate overall transfer-of-value rating
- [ ] Classify the business as weak, average, strong, or best-in-class
- [ ] Recommend valuation adjustment and financing implications

## Four Pillars

**Customer goodwill**: diversified customers, recurring revenue, defensible contracts.

**Employee goodwill**: stable team, capable middle management, low dependency on one technician or owner.

**Structural goodwill**: documented processes, reliable reporting, systems that survive an ownership change.

**Social goodwill**: strong local reputation, referral flywheel, durable brand in the Canadian market.

## Quick interpretation

- Below 45%: weak transferability, likely balance-sheet-value territory or highly creative structure only
- 45% to 58%: buyable with discount and clear improvement plan
- 58% to 72%: healthy lower-middle-market acquisition range
- Above 72%: best-in-class, likely commands premium multiple and heavier competition

## Canada-specific lens

- Provincial licensing and permits matter: HVAC, electrical, plumbing, transport, healthcare, and regulated trades can lose transferability if the owner holds the licence or key relationship personally
- Buyer should verify whether relationships sit with the corporation or with the owner personally, especially in professional services and local referral businesses
- If transferability is weak, recommend seller transition support, staged earnout, or consulting-for-equity before a full purchase

## Output

Provide pillar-by-pillar scores, overall transfer-of-value rating, key value drivers, top transfer risks, valuation impact, and recommended next step.
