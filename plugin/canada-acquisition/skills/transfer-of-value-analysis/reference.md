# Transfer of Value Analysis — Canadian Reference

## Pillar Scoring Guide

Score each factor from 1 to 6.

- 1–2: weak, fragile, heavily owner-dependent
- 3–4: workable, average, but still needs support
- 5–6: strong, transferable, scalable

## Customer Goodwill Factors

| Factor                 | Weak                               | Strong                                        |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Customer concentration | One client over 25% of revenue     | Top client under 10% of revenue               |
| Recurring revenue      | One-off project work only          | Contracts, subscriptions, maintenance plans   |
| Contract quality       | Verbal or short-term relationships | Multi-year agreements or sticky relationships |
| Switching costs        | Easy to replace                    | Operationally painful to switch               |
| Referral strength      | Little repeat or referral business | Strong repeat and referral pattern            |

## Employee Goodwill Factors

| Factor                | Weak                                         | Strong                              |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Key-person dependency | One operator or estimator carries everything | Team can deliver without one person |
| Management depth      | No layer under owner                         | Clear supervisors or GM present     |
| Tenure                | Constant turnover                            | Long-tenured core team              |
| Training              | Informal only                                | Repeatable onboarding and training  |
| Technical bench       | Limited capability outside owner             | Broad capability across staff       |

## Structural Goodwill Factors

| Factor             | Weak                                        | Strong                                       |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| SOPs               | Mostly in owner's head                      | Documented processes                         |
| Reporting          | Poor books, no KPIs                         | Monthly reporting and dashboards             |
| Systems            | Spreadsheets and memory                     | CRM, accounting, scheduling, workflow tools  |
| Owner dependence   | Owner sells, estimates, approves everything | Owner mostly strategic or removable          |
| Handover readiness | No transition plan                          | Clear transition and knowledge transfer path |

## Social Goodwill Factors

| Factor          | Weak                               | Strong                                  |
| --------------- | ---------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Reputation      | Mixed reviews, weak local presence | Strong reviews and community reputation |
| Brand           | Unknown or generic                 | Recognized in the local market          |
| Culture         | Fear, turnover, no trust           | Stable, trusted, low drama team         |
| Market position | Commodity provider                 | Clear niche or specialist position      |

## Transferability Thresholds

- Below 45%: not readily financeable without heavy structure or transition support
- 45% to 58%: financeable only with discount, caution, and a value creation plan
- 58% to 72%: standard buyable range for a good Canadian SME
- Above 72%: premium business, often heavily competed for

## Common Canadian Failure Points

- Owner personally holds key provincial licence or qualification
- Referrals depend on one family name or local relationship
- No second-in-command in trades or professional services
- Major customer is a municipal or crown-related contract not clearly assignable
- Revenue looks recurring but renewal history is weak

## Typical Improvement Levers

- Move customer relationships from owner to team
- Add service agreements or maintenance plans
- Promote or hire a real operations lead
- Implement scheduling, CRM, and KPI dashboards
- Formalize review, referral, and reputation systems
