# Valuing Businesses — Canadian Reference

## Canadian EBITDA Multiple Table

| Normalized EBITDA (CAD) | Base Multiple Range |
| ----------------------- | ------------------- |
| Under $200K             | 1.5x – 2.5x         |
| $200K – $500K           | 2.0x – 3.5x         |
| $500K – $1M             | 2.5x – 4.5x         |
| $1M – $2M               | 3.5x – 5.5x         |
| $2M – $5M               | 4.5x – 7.0x         |

Current Canadian average: ~3.0x–3.5x for lower mid-market. Multiples are trending down.

## Pillar 2 Quality Adjustments (±0.25x per factor)

**Push multiple UP:**

- Recurring / contracted revenue (multi-year) → +0.5x
- Fully owner-independent management team → +0.5x
- Strong barriers to entry (licenses, certs, proprietary processes) → +0.25x
- Customer diversification (no single >15% of revenue) → +0.25x
- Long-term lease with favorable renewal terms → +0.25x
- Strong Google reputation (4.5+ stars, volume) → +0.25x

**Push multiple DOWN:**

- Owner-dependent (owner IS the business) → -0.5x
- Customer concentration >25% → -0.5x
- Project-based / lumpy revenue → -0.25x
- Aging equipment with deferred maintenance → -0.25x
- Declining revenue trend → -0.5x
- High staff turnover → -0.25x

## Pillar 4 Seller Motivation Adjustment

- High motivation (score 40–60 from seller psychology): -0.5x to -1.0x
- Medium motivation (25–39): no adjustment
- Low motivation (below 25): +0.5x to +1.0x

## Share Sale vs. Asset Sale (Canadian)

| Factor            | Share Sale                                                                           | Asset Sale                                         |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Seller preference | Strongly preferred                                                                   | Disfavored                                         |
| Seller tax        | Capital gains; LCGE exempts up to ~$1.25M                                            | Higher effective tax                               |
| Buyer preference  | Disfavored (inherits all liabilities)                                                | Preferred (clean start, step up assets)            |
| Strategy          | Offer slightly lower price for share sale — seller's after-tax proceeds often higher | Demand a discount to compensate for inherited risk |

**LCGE tip**: Offer a share sale at a lower price. Seller often nets more after tax than an asset sale at a higher price. Run the math to show them.

## Example Valuation

Business: HVAC contractor, Ontario. Normalized EBITDA: $650K.

- Base: $500K–$1M range → 2.5x–4.5x
- +0.5x recurring maintenance contracts
- +0.5x licensed techs on staff (owner-independent)
- -0.5x one customer = 28% of revenue
- -0.25x owner doing all sales
- Seller motivation high → -0.75x

**Final range: 2.0x–3.5x**

- Floor: $650K × 2.0x = **$1,300,000**
- Target: $650K × 2.75x = **$1,787,500**
- Ceiling: $650K × 3.5x = **$2,275,000**
- Recommended opening offer: **$1,400,000–$1,600,000**

## Floor / Target / Ceiling Framework

- Floor: price that still works even if minor issues appear in diligence
- Target: fair price for a normal close with balanced terms
- Ceiling: highest justifiable price, usually only if terms and transferability are excellent

## Valuation Discipline Rules

- Never anchor only to asking price
- Do not pay premium multiples for businesses with weak transferability
- Use structure to solve price gaps before simply raising price
- Recheck valuation after diligence if EBITDA, working capital, or concentration assumptions change
